BUSINESS ANALYTICS KNOWLEDGE BASE

Revenue:
Revenue is the total money generated from sales before subtracting business costs.

Profit:
Profit is the amount remaining after subtracting cost from revenue.

Profit Margin:
Profit margin measures how much profit is generated from revenue.
Profit Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100

Regional Analysis:
Regional revenue helps identify which geographic regions generate the highest sales.
Regional profit helps identify which regions contribute the most profit.

Product Analysis:
Top products can be identified by comparing total revenue generated by each product.
A high-revenue product may be important for maintaining sales performance.

Category Analysis:
Comparing profit across product categories helps identify categories contributing strongly to profitability.

Monthly Sales Trend:
Monthly revenue and profit trends help identify increases, decreases, and changes in business performance over time.

Anomaly:
A sales anomaly is a transaction that differs significantly from normal sales behavior.
Examples include unusually high discounts or unusual transaction values.

Heavy Discount:
A heavy discount can reduce profit even when revenue remains high.
Discount-related anomalies should be investigated to determine whether the discount is commercially justified.

Business Decision Guidelines:
High revenue with low profit can indicate pricing problems, high costs, or excessive discounts.

Low revenue with high profit margin can indicate a potentially valuable but smaller business segment.

A region with declining monthly revenue should be investigated for changes in demand, pricing, competition, or customer behavior.

Products with high revenue and high profit are important contributors to business performance.

Anomalous transactions should be investigated before making major business decisions.
